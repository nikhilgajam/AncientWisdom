Understanding Mahāyuga, Spherical Earth & Ahargaṇa మహాయుగం, భూగోళం & అహర్గణం అంటే ఏమిటి?

This opening section introduces the three foundational pillars of the Sūrya Siddhānta:

  • Mahāyuga (4,320,000 Solar Years): A giant cosmic cycle in which all planets complete an exact integer number of revolutions, returning to 0° Meṣa together.
  • Bhūgola (Spherical Earth): Chapter 12 explicitly states Earth is a free-floating sphere (1,600 Yojanas wide) held in space by its own attractive force (Dhāraṇātmikā Śaktiḥ).
  • Ahargaṇa ("Heap of Days"): A continuous integer day count starting from the Kaliyuga epoch (Midnight, Feb 17/18, 3102 BCE) so calendars never drift across centuries.

How to use: Read the constants below—they power every planetary, eclipse, and Nakshatra calculation in the following sections.

Chapter 1 (vv. 51–55) assigns planetary rulers to every scale of time in descending orbital order (Saturn → Jupiter → Mars → Sun → Venus → Mercury → Moon):

  • Dineśa (Day Lord) & Horā (Hour Lord): Every 24-hour day is divided into 24 Horās; the ruler of the 1st Horā at sunrise names the weekday (e.g., Sunday = Sun).
  • Varṣeśa / Māseśa: The planetary lords governing the solar year and 30-day civil month.
  • Bārhaspatya 60-Year Cycle: Traditional 60-year names (Prabhava to Akṣaya) derived from Jupiter's mean motion.

Trigonometric Radius Formulation

Why Radius R = 3438'?

The full circle equals 360° × 60' = 21,600 arcminutes. If Circumference C = 21,600', then Radius R = C / (2π) = 21,600 / (2 × 3.14159265…) ≈ 3437.75' → 3438'. At this radius, linear sine length equals angular arc length for small angles (Jyā(θ) ≈ θ in minutes).

Second-Order Difference Equation

The Recursive Differential Formula

The difference between consecutive sines decreases by the sine value divided by 225:

J(n+1) - J(n) = [J(n) - J(n-1)] - (J(n) / 225)

This is the earliest recorded finite difference representation of d2(sin θ)/dθ2 = −sin θ.
