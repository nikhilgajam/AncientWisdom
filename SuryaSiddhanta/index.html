🪐 1. The Heritage & Architecture of the Cosmos

Revealed according to tradition by the Sun God to Mayāsura at the end of Satya Yuga, the Sūrya Siddhānta represents the zenith of classical Indian mathematical astronomy (*Siddhānta Jyotiṣa*).

Cosmological Geography

Spherical Earth (Bhūgola)

Chapter 12, verse 32 explicitly states that Earth is a spherical ball ("Kanduka-pratimaḥ") hanging unsupported in space, sustained by its own inherent attractive holding power ("Dhāraṇātmikā Śaktiḥ" — ancient Indian formulation of gravity).

Diameter: 1,600 Yojanas • Circumference: ~5,026 Yojanas
Zero Calendar Drift

The Continuous Ahargaṇa

Instead of relying on irregular month names or political calendars that accumulate error, Surya Siddhanta anchors all motion to the Ahargaṇa — a strictly continuous tally of civil days elapsed from the epoch (Feb 18, 3102 BCE).

Civil Days in 4.32M Years: 1,577,917,828
Cosmic Resonance

Human Breath to the Sky

1 Prāṇa (human breath) = 4 sidereal seconds. There are 21,600 prāṇas in one day, matching exactly the 21,600 arcminutes in the 360° celestial sphere! One human breath corresponds to one arcminute of the cosmos turning.

1 Day = 60 Ghaṭikās = 21,600 Prāṇas

📐 5. Trigonometry: The 24-Jyā (Sine) Table

In Chapter 2 (verses 15–22), the Surya Siddhanta presents 24 sines in a 90° quadrant at 225' ($3^\circ 45'$) intervals, computed from radius $R = 3438'$ using an early second-order finite difference equation!

Trigonometric Radius Formulation

Why Radius R = 3438'?

The full circle equals $360^\circ \times 60' = 21,600$ arcminutes. If Circumference $C = 21,600'$, then Radius $R = C / (2\pi) = 21600 / (2 \times 3.14159265...) \approx 3437.75' \rightarrow 3438'$. At this radius, linear sine length matches angular arc length for small angles ($Jy\bar{a}(\theta) \approx \theta$).

Second-Order Difference Equation

The Recursive Differential Formula

The difference between consecutive sines decreases by the sine value divided by 225:

J(n+1) - J(n) = [J(n) - J(n-1)] - (J(n) / 225)

This is the earliest recorded finite difference representation of $d^2(\sin \theta)/d\theta^2 = -\sin \theta$.

⏳ 7. Chronometry: The Nine Systems of Time (Nava-Mānāni)

Chapter 14 classifies the universe into nine distinct operational time scales, from civil commerce to cosmic cycles.

Civil & Commercial Time

1. Sāvana (सावन • సావన)

Terrestrial day measured from sunrise to the next sunrise. Governs civil affairs, wages, fasting durations, and Ahargaṇa tally.

Seasonal & Agricultural Time

2. Saura (सौर • సౌర)

Solar time marked by the Sun entering the 12 zodiac signs (Saṅkrāntis). Regulates the 6 Ṛtus (seasons) and solstices (Ayanas).

Religious & Lunar Time

3. Cāndra (चान्द्र • చాంద్ర)

Measured by Tithis (phases of Moon, 12° elongation intervals) and synodic months. Regulates festivals, vratas, and rituals.

Sidereal & Rotational Time

4. Nākṣatra (नाक्षत्र • నాక్షత్ర)

Earth's rotation relative to fixed stars (1 rotation = 60 nākṣatra ghaṭīs). Regulates planetary velocities and the ascendant (Lagna).

Jovian 60-Year Cycle

5. Bārhaspatya (बार्हस्पत्य • బార్హస్పత్య)

Based on Jupiter's transit through the zodiac asterisms, forming the sacred 60-year Jovian calendar (Prabhava to Kṣaya).

Ancestral Realm Time

6. Paitrya (पैत्र्य • పైత్ర్య)

1 Pitṛ Day = 1 Human Synodic Lunar Month. The dark half (Kṛṣṇapakṣa) is day, and bright half (Śuklapakṣa) is night.

Divine Celestial Time

7. Daiva (दैव • దైవ)

1 Divine Year = 360 Human Solar Years. The Sun's northern path (Uttarāyaṇa) is the day of the Gods; southern path (Dakṣiṇāyana) is night.

Epochal Epoch Time

8. Prājāpatya (प्राजापत्य • ప్రాజాపత్య)

Measures Manvantaras (each 71 Mahāyugas = 306,720,000 solar years) governing eras of patriarchs and cosmic regeneration.

Cosmological Lifespan Time

9. Brāhma (ब्राह्म • బ్రాహ్మ)

1 Kalpa = 1 Day of Brahmā = 4.32 Billion Human Years. Brahmā's lifespan (100 Mahākalpas) = 311.04 Trillion Solar Years!
